# Internet research method

## Purpose

CrowdSmarter will use structured internet research as its main near-term
product-development input. The purpose is to decide which problems merit
attention, which methods have credible support, which capabilities already
exist elsewhere, and which constraints must shape delivery.

Internet research can support a product direction. It cannot demonstrate that
a particular organisation will adopt CrowdSmarter, that participants will
find it usable in practice, or that anyone will pay for it. Those remain
unknown unless later behaviour provides evidence.

## Research questions

Each research cycle should answer a bounded question in one or more of these
areas:

1. **Problem:** What documented failure or burden affects a consequential
   group decision?
2. **Method:** Which participation, facilitation, collective-intelligence, or
   decision practices address that failure, and under what conditions?
3. **Context:** Which current public programmes, policies, implementation
   reports, or procurement records show that the work is active and resourced?
4. **Alternatives:** Which existing services or products already solve the
   problem well enough?
5. **Constraints:** What do connectivity, accessibility, language, privacy,
   safeguarding, and institutional conditions require?
6. **Outcome:** What observable result would justify building, integrating,
   deferring, or rejecting a capability?

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. legislation, regulators, standards, official statistics, public action
   plans, implementation reports, and independent evaluations;
2. peer-reviewed research and method guides from established public-interest
   institutions;
3. official product documentation, repositories, security material, and
   pricing pages for capability comparisons;
4. credible practitioner reports and case studies;
5. vendor marketing, news coverage, and opinion as leads rather than proof.

A vendor page can establish that a feature is offered. It cannot, on its own,
establish that the feature is effective. A policy commitment can establish
intent and a recognised problem. It cannot, on its own, establish successful
implementation or a purchasable market.

## Claim ledger

Record every claim that could change the roadmap with:

- the exact claim in neutral language;
- source, publisher, publication date, and access date;
- source type and jurisdiction;
- whether the evidence is direct or inferred;
- material limitations or conflicting evidence;
- relevance to facilitation, the platform, or operations;
- the decision it supports and what evidence would reverse that decision.

Use these claim states:

- **demonstrated:** directly supported by strong, inspectable evidence;
- **supported:** supported by more than one credible source but not proven in
  CrowdSmarter's own use;
- **plausible:** credible but indirect or based on one source;
- **unknown:** not answerable from the available evidence;
- **contradicted:** credible evidence weighs against the claim.

## Evidence score

Score a roadmap claim out of ten:

| Dimension     | Score | Test                                                                               |
| ------------- | ----: | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Authority     |   0–3 | Is the source responsible for, expert in, or independently evaluating the subject? |
| Directness    |   0–3 | Does it address the claim rather than a neighbouring issue?                        |
| Recency       |   0–2 | Is it current enough for the decision?                                             |
| Triangulation |   0–2 | Is it supported by a materially independent source?                                |

Interpret the total as follows:

- **8–10:** eligible for the roadmap if it fits CrowdSmarter's purpose and
  passes security, privacy, and operational review;
- **6–7:** eligible for a small prototype, facilitation template, or reversible
  experiment, but not a broad product commitment;
- **0–5:** defer, integrate an existing tool, or reject.

No feature should enter the roadmap from a vendor claim alone. No legal
compliance conclusion should be made from the score; jurisdiction-specific
legal advice remains separate.

## Product decision rules

Choose one of four outcomes:

- **Build** when the capability is central to facilitation, supported by strong
  evidence, and not adequately supplied by a sensible integration.
- **Integrate** when a specialist product has greater depth, assurance, data,
  or adoption than CrowdSmarter should reproduce.
- **Defer** when the problem is credible but timing, demand, language scope, or
  operational capacity is uncertain.
- **Avoid** when the capability distracts from facilitation, creates
  disproportionate risk, or is already a commodity.

Prefer the smallest reversible implementation. A printable method, import
format, export, or integration boundary should precede a new application
module when it can answer the same question.

## Review cadence

- Review the claim ledger before each material roadmap phase.
- Refresh time-sensitive sources at least quarterly.
- Recheck laws, regulator guidance, competitor capabilities, pricing, and
  public programmes immediately before relying on them.
- Record a dated decision note whenever research changes the offer, roadmap,
  or deliberate non-goals.
