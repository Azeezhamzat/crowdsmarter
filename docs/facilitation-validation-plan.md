# Facilitation offer evidence plan

## Current approach

CrowdSmarter will use structured internet research as its primary near-term
development input. The work should establish whether a problem is documented,
which methods have credible support, what current programmes and constraints
matter, and whether an existing product should be used instead of building.

The research method is in `docs/research/internet-research-method.md`. The
first dated assessment is in
`docs/research/crowdsmarter-desk-research-baseline-2026-09.md`.

The first evidence cycle tests four hypotheses:

1. Groups struggle to connect participation, evidence, disagreement, and final
   authority across consequential decisions.
2. A bounded facilitation engagement addresses documented problems that
   software access alone does not solve.
3. A connected decision record can support accountability after a facilitated
   process ends.
4. At least one part of the process is sufficiently central and under-served to
   justify CrowdSmarter building it rather than integrating another product.

## Internet research cycle

For each material roadmap question:

1. Write a falsifiable claim and the decision it would change.
2. Search authoritative standards, official statistics, current public plans,
   implementation reports, evaluations, legislation, and regulator guidance.
3. Review official product documentation for alternatives and integration
   boundaries.
4. Record direct evidence, contrary evidence, limitations, jurisdiction, and
   access date in the claim ledger.
5. Score the claim using authority, directness, recency, and triangulation.
6. Choose build, integrate, defer, or avoid.
7. Prefer a reversible facilitation template, import, export, or prototype
   before a new platform module.
8. Publish a dated internal decision record and update the roadmap.

## Evidence record

For each material claim, record:

- the claim and why it matters;
- source, publisher, date, source type, and jurisdiction;
- whether the evidence is direct or inferred;
- evidence for and against the claim;
- the relevant facilitation stage or platform boundary;
- current alternatives and their established capabilities;
- connectivity, accessibility, language, privacy, and operational constraints;
- claim state, score, decision, and reversal condition.

## Initial evidence thresholds

An application capability needs a score of at least 8 out of 10 under the
research method, evidence from more than vendor marketing, a clear link to the
facilitation offer, and no more suitable integration. A score of 6 or 7 permits
only a reversible template or prototype. Lower-scoring work is deferred or
rejected.

Evidence of a public programme, policy commitment, or documented problem does
not establish a customer. Internet research alone cannot clear claims about
adoption, effectiveness, repeat use, or willingness to pay.

## Delivery structure

Any facilitated delivery should begin with a written learning agreement
containing:

- the decision question and named authority;
- the people affected and the intended participation boundary;
- what success and failure would look like;
- the facilitation stages included;
- the platform functions, if any, that will support the work;
- non-digital and assisted participation routes;
- confidentiality, accessibility, safeguarding, and retention requirements;
- the expected outputs and follow-up date;
- permission for any case-study or testimonial use, handled separately from
  delivery consent.

Use `docs/facilitation-field-kit/hybrid-session-capture-pack.md` when
participation crosses in-person, phone, paper, partner-supported, or digital
channels. The platform must not define who can participate.

## Delivery scorecard

Rate each item as demonstrated, partly demonstrated, not demonstrated, or not
applicable, with evidence:

- the decision question and authority were understood;
- participation had a clear and honest purpose;
- people could contribute through an accessible channel;
- relevant knowledge and disagreement influenced the record;
- missing perspectives and important limitations were visible;
- the final judgement could be explained;
- the response stated what changed, what did not change and why;
- follow-through had named ownership;
- participants could use the process without avoidable platform friction;
- the client retained something worth reusing;
- CrowdSmarter's involvement added value beyond an ordinary meeting.

## Review cadence

Review time-sensitive sources quarterly and before each material roadmap
phase. Maintain a short change log covering:

- what was learned;
- which assumption changed;
- what changed in the offer or workflow;
- what evidence would reverse that change.

Do not publish broad outcome or effectiveness claims unless the supporting
evidence can be inspected and any attributable use has been approved.

## Limits and deferred field evidence

The reusable records in `docs/facilitation-field-kit/` remain available if a
real engagement occurs later. Outreach is not a prerequisite for the present
research-led roadmap. However, the absence of field evidence must remain
visible: no public claim should imply demonstrated client demand, participant
usability, outcome improvement, repeat use, or commercial viability.

Keep any completed participant or client records in the agreed confidential
working environment, not in the public source repository.
