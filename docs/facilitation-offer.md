# CrowdSmarter facilitation offer

## Purpose

CrowdSmarter helps a group make a consequential decision through a process
that is participatory, evidence-informed, and accountable. Facilitation is the
service. The platform is used where it helps people prepare, contribute,
deliberate, record the judgement, and carry learning into the next decision.

This document is the working service baseline for research and early
engagements. It should change when the internet-research method or evidence
from real decisions shows that a different approach is more useful.

## The outcome

An engagement should leave the client with:

- a decision question that a named human authority can answer;
- an explicit purpose, scope, participation boundary, and set of criteria;
- the relevant people and forms of knowledge represented in the process;
- a structured record of evidence, assumptions, risks, options, and
  disagreement;
- an attributable decision, including its rationale and any conditions;
- clear commitments, unresolved questions, and next points of accountability;
- a reusable process and lessons for the next decision.

The goal is not consensus at any cost. A valid outcome may preserve minority
positions, objections, or uncertainty while still making responsibility for
the final judgement clear.

## Good-fit decisions

A decision is likely to benefit from CrowdSmarter when most of the following
are true:

- the outcome matters enough to justify a designed process;
- several people hold relevant knowledge, experience, or legitimate interests;
- evidence is incomplete, contested, or distributed across the group;
- the decision has a named authority who is willing to explain the judgement;
- disagreement needs to be understood rather than hidden;
- the group needs a usable record after the meetings end;
- there is enough time to involve people meaningfully before the decision is
  already settled.

CrowdSmarter is a poor fit when the decision has already been made, the stated
participation is purely performative, no one will hold decision authority, an
emergency requires an immediate command response, or the primary need is a
specialist legal, clinical, financial, procurement, or voting system.

## Engagement stages

| Stage | Facilitation work | Client output | Supporting platform role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discover | Understand the decision, urgency, authority, affected people, history, and constraints. | Fit assessment and agreed next step. | No platform setup is required. |
| 2. Frame | Clarify the decision question, purpose, scope, criteria, participation model, and authority. | Decision brief and process design. | Store the governed frame when a shared record adds value. |
| 3. Prepare | Identify participants, evidence needs, accessibility requirements, conflicts, and pre-work. | Participant plan, contribution requests, and session brief. | Invite participants and connect attributable preparation to the decision. |
| 4. Convene | Run structured sessions that surface knowledge, alternatives, uncertainty, and disagreement. | Session outputs and an explicit account of what remains unresolved. | Keep the agenda, guidance, attendance, and named outputs connected. |
| 5. Decide | Help the authorised people compare the record, exercise judgement, and explain the result. | Attributable decision, rationale, conditions, and dissent summary. | Preserve the evidence-to-judgement trail without making the decision. |
| 6. Follow through | Translate the decision into commitments, review points, and learning. | Action ownership, outcome review, and reusable lessons. | Maintain continuity from decision to implementation and the next cycle. |

Not every engagement needs every platform feature. The facilitator should use
the smallest amount of technology that improves participation, traceability,
or continuity.

## Minimum facilitation brief

Before a facilitated session opens, its owner should be able to answer:

1. What decision or question is this session helping to move forward?
2. What must be true by the end of the session?
3. Who is participating, who is observing, and whose perspective is missing?
4. Who holds authority after the session?
5. What preparation, evidence, or accessibility support do people need?
6. How will disagreement and uncertainty be handled?
7. Which outputs will be recorded, by whom, and by when?

The platform session brief therefore includes an objective, agenda,
participation guidance, facilitator, schedule, invited people, attendance, and
linked accountable outputs.

## Delivery formats

The initial offer supports three bounded entry points:

- **Decision discovery:** a focused conversation that establishes whether the
  decision is suitable for a facilitated process and what should happen next.
- **Decision framing:** one or more working sessions that produce a decision
  brief, participation design, and facilitation plan.
- **Facilitated decision engagement:** support from framing through judgement
  and follow-through. A charter grant round is one version of this engagement
  and is normally scoped over 6 to 10 weeks.

The final shape should follow the decision rather than force every client into
the same workshop sequence.

## Practice boundaries

- CrowdSmarter does not make, approve, or silently influence the final
  decision.
- Participation does not imply that every participant has equal authority.
  Authority and influence must be stated honestly.
- Facilitation is not a promise of consensus.
- The platform record does not replace accessible live participation,
  relationship-building, or professional judgement.
- Confidentiality, safeguarding, accessibility, conflicts of interest, and
  data-retention requirements must be agreed before participation begins.
- Specialist systems remain appropriate where the decision depends on legal
  voting, clinical judgement, regulated procurement, payments, or another
  domain-specific control.

## Evidence of value

An engagement should be evaluated against observable evidence rather than a
generic satisfaction score. Useful signals include:

- participants understood the question and how their input would be used;
- relevant perspectives were represented or their absence was recorded;
- the decision authority could explain the evidence, disagreement, and
  rationale;
- commitments and unresolved questions had named owners;
- the client reused part of the process or record in a later decision;
- the supporting platform reduced reconstruction work without becoming a
  barrier to participation.

These measures should be agreed during framing and reviewed after the
decision, not added retrospectively to justify the engagement.
