# Three-conversation discovery sprint

Use this sprint to begin testing CrowdSmarter with real decision experiences.
It produces early evidence, not proof of demand. Do not change the platform in
response to one interesting comment.

## Sprint outcome

By the end of conversation three, you should know:

- whether people can describe a material problem around a real group decision;
- where skilled facilitation would have helped, if at all;
- whether a connected record would have been useful after the meetings;
- which assumption should be tested in the next three conversations;
- whether any live decision deserves a separate fit assessment.

## Choose the first three people

Seek three different viewpoints rather than three people with the same job:

1. **A decision authority** who recently owned or approved a consequential
   group decision.
2. **A process organiser or facilitator** who had to frame, coordinate, or
   document a decision involving several people.
3. **A participant or affected person** whose knowledge or interests needed to
   be represented in a decision they did not control.

Prefer decisions recent enough for the person to remember what actually
happened. Do not recruit only people already enthusiastic about CrowdSmarter.
Record any close personal or professional relationship that could make the
feedback less candid.

Keep contact details in your normal private address book or relationship
system. Use references such as `C01`, `C02`, and `C03` in research records.

## Direct invitation

**Subject:** Could I ask about a recent group decision?

Hello [name],

I am developing CrowdSmarter as a facilitation practice for consequential
group decisions. Before deciding what to build or offer next, I am speaking
with people about decisions they have actually experienced.

Would you be open to a 30-minute conversation about one recent decision: what
was difficult before, during, and after it, and what would have made the
process more useful? This is a research conversation, not a software
demonstration. You would not need to share confidential details, and I would
not attribute a quotation without asking separately.

If you are open to it, please suggest a suitable time. It is also completely
fine to decline.

Thank you,

Azeez

## Short invitation

Hello [name]. I am testing a facilitation approach for consequential group
decisions and would value 30 minutes to learn from a recent decision you
experienced. This is research rather than a software demonstration, and you
would not need to disclose anything confidential. Would you be open to a

## Introduction request

Hello [name],

I am looking for three people willing to discuss a recent consequential group
decision: ideally a decision authority, someone who organised the process, or
a participant whose perspective needed to be represented. The conversation is
research for a facilitation practice, not a software demonstration, and takes

Is there one person you would feel comfortable introducing? Please check with

Azeez

## Opening and consent

Use plain language rather than reading this mechanically:

> Thank you for speaking with me. I am trying to understand how real group
> decisions work before deciding what CrowdSmarter should offer or build. I
> would like to take notes. You can skip any question or stop at any time, and
> there is no need to share confidential or sensitive personal information. I
> will treat comments as unattributed unless we agree otherwise separately.
> Is that acceptable, and do you have any questions before we begin?

This opening records practical agreement for the conversation. It does not
replace any consent, privacy, safeguarding, or research-governance process
required by the setting.

## Thirty-minute agenda

| Time | Focus | Facilitator action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 minutes | Purpose and consent | Confirm the boundaries above and ask for one recent real decision. |
| 3–18 minutes | Decision story | Let the person reconstruct what happened before, during, and after the judgement. Follow their sequence rather than introducing CrowdSmarter. |
| 18–24 minutes | Difficulty and existing response | Explore where time, trust, evidence, participation, authority, or follow-through became difficult and what they tried. |
| 24–27 minutes | Counterfactual | Ask what would have made the process meaningfully better and which parts required a skilled person. |
| 27–29 minutes | Offer reaction | Briefly describe CrowdSmarter as a facilitation practice supported by a connected decision record where useful. Ask what sounds useful, unnecessary, or untrustworthy. |
| 29–30 minutes | Close | Confirm whether follow-up is welcome and agree any next action. Do not ask for a pilot unless a live decision and genuine fit have emerged. |

Use `discovery-conversation-record.md` for the evidence. Do not force every
question into the conversation when the person's account has already answered
it.

## Immediate debrief

Within 15 minutes of each conversation, record:

- the strongest evidence in the participant's account;
- one observation that contradicts the current offer;
- whether the issue required facilitation, software, both, or neither;
- what you inferred and what the participant actually said;
- any follow-up promise and its owner;
- one question to test in the next conversation.

Do not rewrite notes to make the offer look stronger.

## Review after conversation three

Complete one entry in `validation-change-log.md` and answer:

1. Did all three people describe real decisions rather than general opinions?
2. What problem appeared in more than one account?
3. Where did the authority, organiser, and participant perspectives differ?
4. Was facilitation clearly distinct from access to software?
5. Did anyone identify a live decision worth assessing?
6. What evidence suggests reducing the platform's role?
7. What should the next three conversations deliberately vary?

Choose one next action:

- continue with the next three conversations without changing the offer;
- change the recruitment mix because an important viewpoint is missing;
- clarify one part of the offer, then test that revision;
- complete a fit assessment for a live decision;
- pause because the problem or facilitation value is not appearing.

Do not publish a market claim or resume speculative feature development on the
strength of three conversations.
