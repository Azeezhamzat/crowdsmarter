Evidence base

Research-informed by design. The approach draws on commons governance, participatory grantmaking, and collective-intelligence research. Start free as a self-organising group, or bring us a funder's grant round to run as a guided charter programme.

The process

Any group's decision, as a connected system.

Each stage carries evidence forward. The aim is not simply to move a decision through a workflow, whether that decision is a neighbourhood commons choosing how to respond to a flood risk or a funder running a grant round, but to keep participation, assessment, judgement and learning connected from one decision to the next.

  1. 01

    Anticipate

    Define the purpose, who is included, decision criteria and participation model before contribution opens.

  2. 02

    Deliberate

    Bring evidence, member perspectives and reviewer judgement into a structured assessment process.

  3. 03

    Decide

    Compare assessments without concealing disagreement, then record the judgement and evidence behind the final decision.

  4. 04

    Act

    Communicate the outcome, follow through on commitments and establish the next points of accountability.

  5. 05

    Learn

    Carry evidence from this decision into the design of the next one.

Learn→next decision→Anticipate

Connected reasoning

The distinction is continuity, not more software.

Forms, spreadsheets and separate review tools can all be practical. The difficulty appears when evidence, discussion, decision rationale and feedback become separated across them.

When the process is fragmented

Important reasoning can become difficult to reconstruct.

Contributions, assessments, discussion and final decisions may sit in separate tools and records. The resulting path to a decision can be difficult to inspect, and difficult to explain consistently to members, communities or boards.

→

When the reasoning stays connected

Evidence and judgement remain linked across the decision.

CrowdSmarter keeps evidence, assessments, disagreement, the final decision and its rationale connected, creating traceability without removing human judgement.

What stays with you

A decision, and capability that remains.

The value is not a workshop artefact. It is a completed decision process, a documented basis for that decision and a structure the group or organisation can use again.

Methodological foundations

Three disciplines shape how the decision is designed.

CrowdSmarter is not simply grant-management software. Its platform and service model are informed by systems thinking, futures thinking and collective intelligence, each of which changes how evidence, participation and judgement are handled in practice.

Systems thinking

Treat each decision as part of a learning system. Decisions, feedback and observed outcomes inform how eligibility, criteria and participation are designed next time.

Futures thinking

Design criteria around emerging needs and plausible change, rather than assuming that previous funding patterns remain the right guide.

Collective intelligence

Structure multiple forms of knowledge so that community perspectives and reviewer judgement can inform a decision without forcing legitimate disagreement into an artificial consensus.

Governance and trust

Designed to support scrutiny, not obscure it.

Confidence, whether from a neighbourhood commons or an institution, depends on being able to understand who decided, what evidence was considered and what remained under your own control.

01Decision governance
Human decision authority
Final decisions remain with authorised people. CrowdSmarter structures evidence and deliberation; it does not replace accountable judgement.
Traceable decision-making
Assessments, supporting evidence, disagreement and final rationale remain connected so the path to a decision can be inspected later.
Your control
Your commons or organisation retains control of its decision records and can take a documented history of the process with it.
02Platform safeguards

Access, data handling, export requirements and operational controls should be reviewed against your own governance context before a live decision begins.

Security or infrastructure claims are published only where they can be substantiated. Institutions can discuss governance requirements directly as part of a fit assessment.
